Jay Moon, PhD

Applied Scientist & Computational Neuroscientist

I am an applied scientist focused on taking time-series, signal-processing, and anomaly detection models from mathematical formulation into deployed software. I specialize in probabilistic modeling and building reliable ML pipelines for noisy, high-frequency sensor data.

Currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Manitoba, I architect ingestion pipelines and zero-shot feature discovery models for 100Hz continuous neuromonitoring in the ICU. Across my work, from closed-loop BCI prototypes to FDA/Health Canada-approved medical software, I focus on turning shifting real-world signals into objective, production-ready tools.

Selected Work

Time-Series Modeling & Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection

  • Standardized ingestion and quality-control pipelines for high-frequency (100Hz), multi-channel physiological signals across 158 PHIA-compliant neurosurgical ICU datasets.
  • Architected SPARC (CIPO Software Copyright #: 1248739), a standalone visualization and taxonomic artifact-annotation platform validated with Medtronic's Patient Monitoring R&D division.
  • Formulated and validated DASL (Provisional Patent 64/158,894), a statistically generative, zero-shot anomaly detection framework leveraging nonlinear state dynamics to isolate unlabeled failure modes and latent clinical events across shifting reference frames.
  • Awarded Mitacs Lab-to-Market funding to lead technical validation across 36 clinical and industry stakeholders, generating 13 pilot and collaboration requests.

Production Neurotech & Real-Time Closed-Loop Systems

  • At NeuroCatch, authored and deployed data-driven signal-processing and ML algorithms into an FDA/Health Canada-approved EEG Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), raising real-world detection accuracy above 92%.
  • Codified expert clinical criteria (P300, N100, N400 event-related potentials) into an objective system of equations to serve as a reproducible evaluation pipeline, and partnered with engineering to audit drift and failure modes under regulated standards.
  • Previously, during my PhD at the University of Toronto and Holland Bloorview Kids Rehabilitation Hospital, engineered an end-to-end, closed-loop EEG brain-computer interface (BCI) prototype. Using a passive speech-perception training paradigm to overcome labeled-data scarcity, the system achieved up to 93% online classification accuracy under strict real-time inference constraints.

Scientific Advisory & Technical Due Diligence

  • Advised management consulting and product teams (McKinsey & Co., De:Mind, Card79) as a Subject Matter Expert on sensor hardware constraints, signal reliability, and real-world feasibility across invasive and non-invasive neurotechnology systems.
